# The Horizon-2 Smooth and Stochastic Foundations {#sec:horizon2_smooth_stochastic}

The kernel described so far is finite. H1 closed on a shared sixteen-state Boolean carrier, and every result quoted for it is a statement about finite laws, finite kernels, and finite sums. Horizon 2 is the continuation of that program onto smooth and stochastic carriers, and its foundations are inside the same maintained manifest — they are counted in the {{formalism.metrics.formal_modules}} modules and {{formalism.metrics.formal_resource_theorems}} declarations reported above, so the reader who has been tracking those numbers has already been reading H2 modules without seeing them named. This section names them and states what each proves.

The pattern is the one used throughout: a carrier is selected narrowly enough that the theorem is exact, and the boundary is stated in the same breath as the result.

## Posterior convergence {#sec:h2_posterior_convergence}

`FEP.PosteriorConvergence` is the module that carries asymptotic Bayesian learning. Its carrier is a two-hypothesis Gaussian-mean model with a positive prior on each hypothesis, sampled jointly with an i.i.d. observation path. On that carrier the module proves that the posterior probability of a fixed hypothesis is a martingale for the observation filtration (`posteriorProbability_martingale`), that it converges almost everywhere (`posteriorProbability_tendsto_ae`), and — after identifying the limiting observation with the sampled parameter — that the limit is the indicator of the truth (`posteriorProbability_consistent_ae`). Two consequences follow: the parameter posterior converges weakly to the Dirac law at the sampled mean (`parameterPosterior_tendsto_dirac_ae`), and the bounded zero-one Bayes risk of the induced report tends to zero (`posteriorDecisionRisk_tendsto_zero_ae`).

This is posterior contraction, proved rather than assumed, and it is the reason [@sec:semantic_closure_validation] scopes the kernel's negative claim to minimax, empirical calibration, and generic marginal-likelihood optimization rather than to contraction as such. What it is not is a contraction theorem for any richer parameter space, for the Laplace estimator of the finite-sample risk family, or for a model the data were not drawn from. `finitePosterior_eventualContraction_regression` in the same module pins the H1 finite boundary so the two carriers cannot be silently merged, and `FEP.FinitePosteriorLearning` keeps the finite analogues (`empiricalLogLikelihoodRatio_strongLaw`, `posteriorBadMass_eventually_contracts`) separate.

## Native semigroups and exact Gaussian transition families {#sec:h2_semigroups}

`FEP.MarkovSemigroup` defines a native kernel semigroup over a measurable state space, with `InvariantLaw` and `ReversibleLaw` predicates, and proves the two structural facts the later modules consume: reversibility implies invariance (`reversibleLaw_invariantLaw`), and KL to an invariant law is nonincreasing along the semigroup (`nativeKL_to_invariant_nonincrease`). It also lifts a finite Markov semigroup and an action-indexed one into that native form, which is how the finite H1 carriers reach the smooth layer.

Three modules then supply exact instances rather than existence claims. `FEP.ScalarGaussianSemigroup` constructs the scalar Ornstein--Uhlenbeck transition family, proves its stationary law is invariant (`stationaryLaw_invariant`), computes the transition mean and variance in closed form (`ouTransition_mean`, `ouTransition_variance`), and proves convergence to the invariant law both weakly and in KL (`ouTransitionProbability_tendsto_invariant`, `ouKL_to_stationary_nonincrease`). `FEP.LinearGaussianSemigroup` does the same on a finite axis set with a matrix drift, closing the same convergence statements (`transitionProbability_tendsto_invariant`) and proving that the one-axis specialization is definitionally the scalar case (`finOneTransition_eq_scalarOU`). `FEP.Fin4GaussianSemigroup` is the four-axis symmetric-precision specialization, where the parameters are exact (`scalarParameters_exact`) and the projected transition again reduces to the scalar OU family (`projectedTransition_eq_scalarOU`).

Every one of these is a statement about a selected Gaussian family. None of them is a general theory of continuous-state stochastic dynamics, and none licenses reading the OU relaxation as physical dissipation.

## Precision conditioning and the smooth blanket boundary {#sec:h2_precision_conditioning}

`FEP.GaussianPrecisionConditioning` is where the blanket question moves off the finite carrier. It works with the stationary joint law of the Fin4 family and proves the conditional factorizations directly, including the endpoint `CondIndepFun` statement that the finite `FEP.MarkovBlanket` foundation could only state finitely. It then constructs a fixed bivariate precision perturbation and proves the resulting endpoints are *not* independent (`perturbedEndpoint_external_not_indep_internal`), with the actual covariance computed rather than bounded (`perturbedEndpointCovariance_external_internal`). The negative result is the point: it fixes the boundary of the blanket construction on a smooth carrier instead of leaving it to interpretation.

## Smooth information geometry {#sec:h2_information_geometry}

`FEP.GaussianInformationGeometry` builds the fixed-variance Gaussian family as a genuine statistical manifold: the law is `gaussianReal` (`law_eq_gaussianReal`), it has a density against volume, laws at different means are mutually absolutely continuous (`law_mutuallyAbsolutelyContinuous`), the KL between two laws is the scaled squared mean difference (`klDiv_law_eq_meanSquare`), and the Fisher information in the mean coordinate is the inverse variance (`meanFisher_eq_inv_variance`). Dual coordinates are then real: `klDiv_law_eq_naturalBregman` identifies the KL with the Bregman divergence of the natural potential, and `meanFisher_eq_naturalFisher_pullback` relates the two metrics.

`FEP.SmoothInformationGeometry` carries the local, coordinate-level statements and one deliberate no-go: a duplicated mean parameterization has a null tangent direction (`duplicatedNullTangent_ne_zero`, `duplicatedMeanPullbackMetric_null`) and its pullback metric is therefore not positive definite (`duplicatedMeanPullback_not_positiveDefinite`). Multidimensional smooth exponential-family geometry with dual connections is out of scope, and that no-go is the reason it is stated as out of scope rather than assumed to follow.

## Composed smooth agents {#sec:h2_composed}

Four composition modules connect the foundations above. `FEPComposed.GaussianFilter` builds an evidence-almost-everywhere native Gaussian filter: the prediction belief is the OU transition (`predictionBelief_law_eq_ouTransition`), the evidence law is Gaussian in closed form (`evidenceLaw_eq_gaussian`), and the chronological recursion is proved by its two equations (`filterRecursion_nil`, `filterRecursion_cons`). `FEP.DecisionRisk` supplies the decision layer, including a strict separation between a revealing and a garbled observation channel (`revealingBool_bayesRisk_lt_garbled`), and `FEPComposed.GaussianControl` consumes one transition to select an action with strictly better risk (`boolWitness_true_strictlyBetter`, `boolWitness_selectedAction`). `FEPComposed.GaussianGridPath` proves the monotone finite-grid path law identities with their support and integrability boundaries made explicit (`gridPathKL_eq_expectedLogRatio`, `gridPathKL_eq_top_of_not_ac`, `gridPathKL_eq_top_of_not_integrable`). `FEPComposed.SmoothReferenceKernel` is the terminal composition (`smoothReferenceKernel_terminal`, `fin4ReferenceKernel_terminal`).

## The Horizon-2 boundary {#sec:h2_boundary}

Horizon 2 does not establish an unrestricted FEP on continuous state spaces. Its Gaussian families are selected, its posterior-contraction carrier has two hypotheses, its information geometry is one-dimensional and fixed-variance, and its blanket results hold for one stationary joint and one named perturbation. Nothing in this section is empirical evidence: it is deductive content about the maintained formal resources, and the compilation state of those resources for this render is reported separately in [@sec:quantitative_execution_metrics].
